Triad

Compare landing sites and dates at the Moon's south pole: where the Sun keeps a lander powered and where Earth stays in view for direct communication.

Team Ultimate Laplace · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Data: NASA LRO LOLA terrain, NAIF SPICE ephemerides, checked against JPL Horizons.

4 · Horizon, Sun and Earth

The terrain horizon all around the site (north at 0°, east at 90°). The yellow dots are the Sun's path on the chosen day and the blue dots are Earth's. The Sun (yellow) and Earth (blue) are drawn at the chosen time at their true size; heights are stretched so that the terrain can be seen, which is why the disks look tall.

Accuracy

  • Sun and Earth directions match JPL Horizons within 0.00003° (40 checks at 4 sites on 5 dates).
  • The terrain map is read without a pixel shift (checked against Shackleton crater and against a second LOLA map).
  • The Moon's atmosphere is ignored for refraction: its surface pressure is about 3 × 10⁻¹⁵ bar (NASA Moon Fact Sheet).
  • Results use 10-minute steps; times of sunrise, sunset and Earth rise can be off by up to 10 minutes.
